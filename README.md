# When Does LNG Connect the World? Physical Tightness and Nonlinear Price Discovery Across Henry Hub, TTF and JKM

This project studies how the major regional natural-gas benchmarks Henry Hub, TTF and JKM interact through the global LNG market. With particular attention to whether price transmission changes when physical gas markets become tight.

## Core Research Question

Does the transmission of price shocks across Henry Hub, TTF and JKM become stronger or weaker when regional gas markets are physically constrained?

The project investigates whether relationships between these benchmarks are nonlinear: relatively weak under normal market conditions, but substantially different when storage is low, LNG demand is high, or liquefaction and transportation capacity become constrained.

Particular attention will be given to the JKM–TTF spread, which captures competition between Northeast Asia and Europe for flexible LNG cargoes, and to the relationship between international LNG prices and Henry Hub, the major U.S. natural-gas benchmark.

## Initial Hypotheses

1. TTF and JKM should become more sensitive to one another during periods of physical tightness.
    -  This is because Euroxpe and Asia compete for marginal LNG cargoes.

2. Large JKM–TTF spreads should create incentives to redirect flexible LNG cargoes toward the higher-priced region subject to freight, regasification and contractual constraints.

3. Henry Hub should behave differently from TTF and JKM. 
    - It represents U.S. pipeline gas rather than delivered LNG, so the connection between Henry Hub and international LNG prices depends on U.S. liquefaction capacity and export demand.

4. Price transmission may therefore be nonlinear. 
    - A $1/MMBtu international price shock may have very different effects when inventories are comfortable than when storage or infrastructure is scarce.
